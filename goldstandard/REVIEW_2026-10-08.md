## PRESS 1 — `supplier_gln` → `NO_MAPPING`. I would challenge this.

Your rationale: genuinely ambiguous between `manufacturerOperatorId`,
`otherOperatorIdentifiers` and `facilityIdentifiers`, and a supplier need not be
the manufacturer. R4.3.

**The counter-argument an examiner would make.** ESPR Annex III 1(g) reads
"information related to the manufacturer, **such as its unique operator
identifier**". GS1's own DPP application standard maps *unique operator
identifier* → **GLN**. A column literally named `supplier_gln` holding a
13-digit GLN is the canonical instance of that clause.

The supplier-versus-manufacturer distinction is real, but it is a **data-quality
caveat**, not a mapping ambiguity: in a garment supply chain the supplier of the
export file *is* the manufacturer in the overwhelming majority of cases, and
`otherOperatorIdentifiers` exists precisely for the ones who are not.

**What it costs you.** `manufacturerOperatorId` is mandatory, and this is the
only operator column in either schema. Mapping it takes Schema B from 3/4 to
**4/4** mandatory coverage and Schema C from 2/4 to **3/4**.

**My view:** map it to `manufacturerOperatorId` and state the
supplier≠manufacturer caveat in the rationale. But R4.3 is a legitimate call and
you may keep it — just be ready for the question, and say in Chapter 6 that
`manufacturerOperatorId` is never populated by design of the ground truth, not
by matcher failure.

## PRESS 2 — `item_group` / `categ_id` → `productCategory`. Check the description.

You flagged this yourself: *"If that field is a controlled delegated-act category
list, switch to NO_MAPPING under R4.3."* Good instinct. Here is the answer.

The schema says:

> `productCategory`: *product classification, **such as a UN-CPC or similar
> scheme code*** — source: UNTP `credentialSubject.productCategory`

"Knitwear" and "All / Apparel / Knit" are **free-text labels, not scheme codes**.
By R3.3 — a label is not an identifier — the stricter reading is `NO_MAPPING`.

**Counter-argument for keeping it:** UNTP's `productCategory` is an array of
classification objects with an `idValue` *and* a `name`; a human-readable
category name is a legitimate member. "Similar scheme code" is doing loose work
in that description.

**Either answer is defensible. What is not defensible is deciding it differently
in B than in C** — and you did keep them consistent, so whichever way you go,
change both.

## PRESS 3 — `Style_No` → `gtin` leaves Schema A with no `productIdentifier`

Your reasoning is sound: the values are 13-digit and pass the GTIN-13 check
digit, R4.1 says prefer the more specific field, and `gtin`'s description says
exactly that. Schema C's `barcode` → `gtin` is consistent with it.

**The consequence to be ready for.** `productIdentifier` is mandatory and
nothing in Schema A maps to it. Annex III treats them as separate points —
1(b) unique product identifier, 1(c) GTIN — so an examiner may ask why a
mandatory field is never populated.

The answer is good: *the schema supplies a trade item number rather than a
separate product identifier, and R1.1 forbids mapping one column to two fields.*
Have it ready.

## CODE BUG your decisions exposed — Metric 2 was computing the wrong thing

Your mandatory coverage looked alarming:

| Schema | Mandatory fields present in the data |
|---|---|
| `manufacturer_a` | 2/4 — missing `productIdentifier`, `manufacturerOperatorId` |
| `manufacturer_b_erpnext` | 3/4 — missing `manufacturerOperatorId` |
| `manufacturer_c_odoo` | 2/4 — missing `productFamily`, `manufacturerOperatorId` |

Against methodology.md's Metric 2 target of **≥80% per schema**, that looked
unreachable. But methodology.md defines Metric 2 as *"restricted to fields the
source schema actually has data for"* — and `eval/metrics.py` was dividing by
**all** mandatory fields, ignoring the restriction.

Two different denominators, two different numbers, and the code was computing
the one your methodology does not describe.

**Fixed.** `mandatory_match_rate()` now takes the gold standard and restricts
the denominator to mandatory fields the ground truth maps something to. A new
`mandatory_coverage_ceiling()` reports the unrestricted figure alongside,
because the ceiling is itself a finding: it measures SME data completeness
rather than artefact performance, and 50–75% is a result worth reporting in its
own right.

Both now appear in every run record. **Report both**, and say which is Metric 2.

## CONSEQUENCE — Schema C cannot produce a DPP at all

`manufacturer_c_odoo` has no `productFamily`: Odoo's `name` went to
`NO_MAPPING` (correctly, R3.3 — it is free text, and B's `variant_of` held a
code while C has no family-code column at all).

I ran it. Schema A and B both generate a DPP; **Schema C returns HTTP 422** —
"No family-level identifier was mapped. ADID requires a product family…"
That is the D9 guard working as designed, refusing to use a GTIN as a family.

For **Phase 5a Demonstration** (D14), use Schema A or B — C genuinely cannot
complete the pipeline. That is not a bug to fix; it is a finding:

> A schema with no family-level identifier cannot be assigned an ADID, because
> ADID's family level is not derivable from variant-level data. This is the
> practical consequence of D9's identity/data separation and belongs in Chapter 6
> as a stated limitation of the approach for sources organised purely at variant
> level — which Odoo's `product.template` export is.

That paragraph is worth more than a clean result.

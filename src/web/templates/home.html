Open any product and four kinds of connection appear next to it. Choose one to see it on the map.

Every connection follows the same access rules as a direct lookup, so individual shirts are never listed publicly and batch details stay private.

From spreadsheet to printed label

  1. Upload

    The product sheet you already keep, as CSV, Excel or JSON.

  2. Match

    The gateway matches your column names to passport fields, each with a confidence score.

  3. Check

    You confirm or correct each match. Your choices can be saved as a template.

  4. Register

    Each design, variant and serialised item receives its ADID and passport record.

  5. Print

    Put the ADID in the label's QR code. Later corrections update the record behind it.

The regulation this responds to

Passport data must be transferable "without vendor lock-in".
EU Regulation 2024/1781, Art. 10(1)(d)

ADIF uses open identifiers, an open data model and JSON-LD, not one supplier's product codes.

Passports may be required at model, batch or item level.
EU Regulation 2024/1781, Art. 9(2)(d)

ADID addresses the design, variant and item, and records the batch as passport data.

Textiles, in particular garments, are a priority product group.
EU Regulation 2024/1781, Art. 18(5)(c)

The textile delegated act is not yet adopted, so ADIF does not claim compliance with requirements that do not exist yet.
